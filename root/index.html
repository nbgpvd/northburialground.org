North Burial Ground Foundation

An independent nonprofit being organized to support the preservation, maintenance, improvement and study of North Burial Ground, in coordination with the City of Providence.

The Foundation is not the cemetery. North Burial Ground is Providence’s municipal cemetery — about 110 acres, more than 100,000 people, in use since 1700 and still active — and it is owned and operated by the City. The Foundation is a separate body, newly organized to support it. This site is the Foundation’s.

What the Foundation is for

  • Care of the grounds Tools, equipment and volunteer hands for the people who keep the cemetery, and for the repair and resetting of stones.
  • Study of the people buried here Documented, cited research into the lives recorded on the stones, published free for anyone standing in front of one.
  • Finding your way A cemetery of this size is hard to read on foot. Maps, routes and plain labeling, so a visitor can find a grave and know what they are looking at.
  • Gathering at the cemetery Support for public events on the grounds, beginning with the annual Juneteenth observance at the Prescott Post lot.

The registry

The Foundation’s long work is a register of the people buried here: a page for each person and grave, drawn from the City’s own lot records and from the surveys made of the stones, with the documents shown beside the life. Tours and research projects are then routes and readings across that register, not separate efforts — a man can belong to several without being written up twice.

It will be built a lot at a time, and it begins with the Prescott Post lot.

Other parts of the ground will follow as the register reaches them: the Free Ground and the Friends’ Ground, the oldest sections, and the lots that the City’s own walking tours already visit.

Where things stand

The Foundation is being organized and is not yet incorporated. It is not accepting donations until it is. This page will carry the mission, board, giving and contact information once they exist.

In the meantime the most useful help is with the research: photographs of stones, family papers, and records of the men in the Prescott Post lot. See how to help.
