# How to help

About three hundred Union veterans lie in this lot. We are trying to learn who each of them was, from the records, and to put what we learn at the stone where a visitor can read it. Most of the work is still ahead of us. Three kinds of help make the most difference.

## 1. Photograph the stones

{{photo_counts}}

Every photograph must come with its **grid position**: the row letter and column number from the [plot map](../map/index.html). A1 is the top-left stone as you face the rows from the road. Put it in the filename (`F5_2026-11-08.jpg`) or in a note with the upload. If you are not sure, photograph the neighbouring stones as well and we will work it out.

How to shoot:

- Straight on, filling the frame; then a second, wider shot showing the stone in its row.
- Soft light is best: an overcast day, or early morning or late afternoon when the sun rakes across the letters. Full midday sun flattens them.
- Brush grass aside by hand if it covers letters.
- **Please do not dig, pull turf, scrub, chalk, or put anything on a stone.** If a stone is sunk or overgrown, photograph it as it is and tell us. The City's cemetery staff will clear it properly.
- Add your name so we can credit you.

## 2. Find a record of a man

Each man's page ends with an **Upload a record** button. It takes a census page, a pension card, a city-directory entry, a newspaper item, a photograph, a family story — anything that shows who he was. You do not need an account. With each file, tell us what it is, where you found it, and why you believe it is this man: a matching name is not a matching man, and that reasoning is the most valuable thing you can give us. Every contributor is credited on the page.

{{pages_with_requests}}

## 3. Tell us if you are family

If you are descended from a man buried here, or know someone who is, please say so when you upload, or write to us. We do not publish a page about a man whose descendants we know of without talking to them first, and family papers and memory are often the only route past what the government wrote down.

## What happens to what you send

Uploads go to a holding folder that only the project's researchers can see. We read each file, check the identity, and record where it came from. Records we can host — National Archives files, City of Providence records, your own photographs — go on the man's page with a caption and a citation. Records from subscription sites are cited and linked, not copied. Nothing is published without a source.
