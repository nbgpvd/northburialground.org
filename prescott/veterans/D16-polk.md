# Polk



## Life

The stone in grave D16 of the Prescott Post lot has been read only as far as POLK; the man it names is not yet identified. No date of death has been read from it. [1]

The PV001 list has a James H. Polk of the 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery, died 1910, and the index has a James K. Polk in the 11th U.S. Colored Heavy Artillery. Until the stone is raised and read, he is a candidate, not an identification. [2]

A 2022 list of Civil War veterans buried at North Burial Ground gives his death as 1910. [3]

His service record, any pension file, and his life before and after the war have not yet been researched. This page will grow as the records are read.

## What we don't know

- Only the surname has been read. The marker is sunk; the City's staff would need to clear it before the rest can be read.
- What exactly does the stone say? It has not yet been photographed; the name, unit and date here are from the survey sheet.
- When and where did he enlist, and what did his company do? His compiled military service record (National Archives, RG 94) will say.
- Did he or his widow apply for a pension? The General Index to Pension Files (NARA T288/T289) has not yet been searched for him.
- Where was he born, and how did he come to Providence?
- Where did he live and work in Providence? The city directories and the censuses of 1870–1920 have not yet been read for him.
- Did he marry, and did he leave children?
- When was he buried here, and what does the City's interment record say? The City lot record has not yet been matched.
