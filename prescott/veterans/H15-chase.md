# Joseph N. Chase

9th U.S. Colored Infantry

## Life

Joseph N. Chase lies in grave H15 of the Prescott Post lot. His stone names his unit as 9th U.S. Colored Infantry, and gives his death as June 1 of a year in the 1880s, the last digit not read. [1]

The National Park Service's index of Civil War soldiers lists Chase, Joseph (no middle initial), 9th U.S. Colored Infantry, Company H, private in and out, microfilm M589 roll 16 — a probable match. [2]

The 9th was raised from Black men of Maryland at Camp Stanton, Benedict, Maryland, in November 1863. [3] Maryland's printed roster of the regiment lists a Private Joseph Chase in Company H, the company on the stone. He enlisted on November 22, 1863, and was discharged on June 8, 1865, on account of wounds received in action on September 30, 1864. [3] The roster gives no middle initial, so this is still a probable match until his service record is read.

His service record, any pension file, and his life before and after the war have not yet been researched. This page will grow as the records are read.

## What we don't know

- He is not on the PV001 list.
- Where was he wounded on September 30, 1864? The roster gives the date of the wound but not the place. [3]
- When and where did he enlist, and what did his company do? His compiled military service record (National Archives, RG 94) will say.
- Did he or his widow apply for a pension? The General Index to Pension Files (NARA T288/T289) has not yet been searched for him.
- Where was he born, and how did he come to Providence?
- Where did he live and work in Providence? The city directories and the censuses of 1870–1920 have not yet been read for him.
- Did he marry, and did he leave children?
- When was he buried here, and what does the City's interment record say? The City lot record has not yet been matched.
