# JAMES B. BERRY

Co. A, 39th U.S. Colored Infantry

## Life

James B. Berry lies in grave M23 of the Prescott Post lot. His stone names his unit as Company A of the 39th U.S. Colored Infantry, and gives his death as an April day of a year now illegible on the stone. [1]

No James B. Berry was found in the index for the 39th U.S. Colored Infantry. The index has a James B. Berry in the 128th U.S. Colored Infantry, not yet checked. [2]

The 39th was raised at Baltimore in March 1864 from Black men across Maryland. [3] Maryland's printed roster of the regiment lists no James B. Berry in Company A. It does list three other Berrys there, all enlisted in March 1864: Corporal John Berry, discharged June 24, 1865; Private Joseph T. Berry, mustered out December 4, 1865; and Private William H. Berry, missing in action at the Crater, July 30, 1864. [3] Whether the man in this grave is one of them, or a man the roster missed, is not yet known.

His service record, any pension file, and his life before and after the war have not yet been researched. This page will grow as the records are read.

## What we don't know

- **Is the name on the stone read right?** The surname is worn; only its first two letters and possibly a final Y are clear in the photograph. The roster has no James B. Berry in Company A. A raking-light photograph and his service record are needed. [1][3]
- When and where did he enlist, and what did his company do? His compiled military service record (National Archives, RG 94) will say.
- Did he or his widow apply for a pension? The General Index to Pension Files (NARA T288/T289) has not yet been searched for him.
- Where was he born, and how did he come to Providence?
- Where did he live and work in Providence? The city directories and the censuses of 1870–1920 have not yet been read for him.
- Did he marry, and did he leave children?
- When was he buried here, and what does the City's interment record say? The City lot record has not yet been matched.
