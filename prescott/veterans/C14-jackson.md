# ISRAEL JACKSON

Private, Co. F, 1st U.S. Colored Infantry. Mason. Born Maryland or Washington, D.C., about 1843. Died Providence, July 31, 1913.

## Life

Israel Jackson was born in the early 1840s. The Army wrote down Montgomery County, Maryland; in later life he gave Washington, D.C. His parents were Felix and Emily Jackson. [2][6][7]

On June 21, 1863, at Mason's Island in the Potomac at Washington, he enlisted for three years in Company F of the 1st U.S. Colored Infantry, under Colonel William Birney. He was nineteen, a waiter, five feet seven. [2] His regiment went on to Petersburg and New Market Heights in 1864 and to Fort Fisher and Wilmington in 1865. [16] At some point he was wounded in the right hip by a piece of shell. [3] In May 1865 his papers were sent to the hospital at Raleigh; they came back in June. He was mustered out at Roanoke Island, North Carolina, on September 29, 1865. [2]

By the early 1870s he was in Providence, a mason, living at 6 Lilac Street with his wife Rebecca. The two opened a savings account together. [4] The city directories list him as a mason at 6 Lilac Street through the 1880s. [5] In 1890 he was still on Lilac Street, where several other Black veterans lived, and he told the census taker about his wound. [3]

On December 10, 1890, a widower working as a teamster, he married Melvina H. Beams, a widow born in Baltimore. An A.M.E. pastor performed the service. [6] In 1900 they lived at 63 Van Zandt Street with her son William Beams, a barber. Israel did job work. Melvina had borne five children; one was living. [7][8]

From 1903 the directories have him a mason again, at 93 Wadsworth Street, and they keep him there through 1910. [9][10][11][12]

He died on July 31, 1913. [13]

White marble, lying flat and sunk at the foot, so that the lower part of the second line and any date line are buried. Lettering filled with soil; the name and the first line of the unit are legible. Photograph IMG_1297 (B. Brennan). [1]

The stone's "1 U.S. COL. TR." and the Army's "1 U.S. Col'd Inf." name the same regiment. [2] The register and survey sheet read the stone as "Col. Inf."; the photograph reads "COL. ## What we don't know

- **His birth year and birthplace.** The Army took him at 19 in June 1863; he was 47 in December 1890 and "July 1843" in 1900. But the bank clerk of the early 1870s wrote "37 yrs old," which would put his birth about 1835. The Army said Montgomery County, Maryland; his marriage and the 1900 census say Washington, D.C. The tree's "2 Feb 1844" has no record behind it in the working folder. [2][4][6][7][15]
- **Whether he was born enslaved.** No record seen says so. The 1860 slave schedule attached to the tree is not his: it names a slaveholder called Israel Jackson in Prince George's County, Maryland. That man held six people, one of them a boy of sixteen, the age our Israel was that June. Whether that is coincidence or the origin of his surname is a question, not a finding. The 1865 tax list of Hyattsville (Israel Jackson Sr. and Jr., with carriages and a piano) is the same slaveholding family, and the tree's "1865 residence, Maryland" rests on it; in 1865 our man was in North Carolina with his regiment. [2][17][18]
- **Where and when he was wounded.** The 1890 schedule says "right hip by piece of shell" and gives no place. The register's note of a leg wound at Petersburg has no source in the working folder. The 14 unread cards of his service record (muster rolls, hospital and casualty entries) should answer this. [2][3]
- **His pension.** The jacket's reverse carries pencilled numbers that look like pension references. No General Index to Pension Files card has been found yet, and no file has been ordered. [2]
- **His first wife, Rebecca A. Jackson.** She signed the bank book with him in the early 1870s, aged 39. Her maiden name, their marriage, and her death (before December 1890, when he was a widower) are not found. [4][6]
- **The 1890 marriage against the 1900 census.** The register has the marriage on December 10, 1890; the census says the couple had been married seven years, which would be 1893. The register is weighted as the record made at the time. [6][7]
- **Melvina's children.** Five born, one living in 1900. William Beams, 30, a barber born in Maryland, was in the house as "son." [7]
- **His death.** The register and tree give July 31, 1913; the survey list (PV001) gives 1905; the stone's date line is in the ground. The tree's sources (VA Master Index card, Find a Grave, the Rhode Island Historical Cemetery Commission index) are not in the working folder; the death return has not been seen. [13][15]
- **When he was buried in this plot,** and the City's ledger entry.
- **The undated directory pages.** Four pages (pp. 138, 149, 178, 218) carry no year; by their page numbers they fall between about 1870 and 1883. Confirm the years from Ancestry's image data. One page (p. 252) is in the folder twice as two scans. [5]
- **Whether "J." stands for James.** Only the 1890 marriage register spells it out. [6]
- **Felix and Emily Jackson.** His parents are named once, in 1890. No record of them has been looked for. [6]
- **Lilac Street.** The 1890 schedule lists him on the same page as Charles C. Ringgold, Jefferson White, Anthony Shepard and Ambrose Clark of the 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery, who lies in this plot at L24. Whether they were neighbors by chance or by community is for the city directories to show. [3]
