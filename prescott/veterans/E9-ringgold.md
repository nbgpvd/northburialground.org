# BENJAMIN RINGGOLD

Corporal, Co. G, 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery (Colored) · c. 1840–1909

## Life

Benjamin Ringgold was born in Baltimore, Maryland, about 1840. [2][12] He was a farmer, twenty-five years old, when he enlisted at Providence in October 1863 in Company G of the 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery, a regiment of Black soldiers that the War Department later renamed the 11th United States Colored Heavy Artillery. [2][4] He was made a corporal on June 23, 1865, and mustered out on October 2, 1865. [3][10]

By 1870 he was living in Providence with his wife Louisa, who was born in Virginia. [5] They had no children. [11] He worked as a domestic servant in 1870, a steward in 1875, a life insurance agent in 1880, a janitor in 1885 and a waiter in 1900. [5][6][7][9][11] In June 1885 he and Louisa opened a joint account at the Providence Institution for Savings; they then lived at 17 Walker Street. [9] In 1890 he told the census that he had come out of the war with kidney disease. [10] By 1900 the couple were renting at 55 South Main Street and taking in boarders. [11]

Louisa died before him. [12] He died at 55 South Main Street on January 5, 1909, at about seventy, of tuberculosis, and was buried at North Burial Ground. [12] The city's register of his death gives his name as "Benjamin Johnson or Ringgold" and his parents as Robert and Hannah Johnson. No other record found so far explains the second name. [12]

## What we don't know

- When exactly was he born? The descriptive book (25 in October 1863) and the death register (70 in January 1909) put it about 1838; the censuses of 1870–1885 put it 1840–1842; the 1900 census alone says November 1832. [2][5][6][7][9][11][12]
- Why does the 1909 death register call him "Benjamin Johnson or Ringgold" and name his parents as Robert and Hannah Johnson? [12] Was Johnson a birth name, a family name, or a name he used in Providence? A pension file, if one exists, is where he or Louisa would have been asked to explain it. No pension index card has yet been found; the index should be searched under both surnames.
- Was he free or enslaved in Baltimore before the war? A free Black household headed by a Benjamin Ringgold appears in the 1840 census of Baltimore's 8th Ward, and a Benjamin Ringgold is indexed in Baltimore County in 1860; neither has been tied to him, and the Johnson parentage weighs against assuming a connection. [13][14]
- A Benjamin Ringgold, 24, Black, laborer, married, born Maryland, was registered for the draft at Quakertown, Bucks County, Pennsylvania, in June 1863, four months before our man enlisted at Providence. [15] Is this him? If so, he had a wife before Louisa. The record does not settle it.
- When was he enlisted? The descriptive book says October 20, 1863; the Register of Rhode Island Volunteers gives October 29 (mustered November 4); he told the 1890 census October 17. [2][3][10]
- What did Company G do in the war, and where was he? His compiled service record beyond the descriptive book card, and Chenery's regimental history, have not yet been read against his dates. [2][13]
- When did Louisa die, and where is she buried? Her birth is given as Orange County, Virginia, maiden name Smith, about 1841; her age varies across the censuses. [5][6][7][9][11]
- Where did the couple live in 1875 and 1880? The street names are on the census pages but have not yet been read. [6][7]
- Did he belong to a Grand Army post, and did the post bury him?
- What does the City's interment record for January 1909 say, and under which name?
