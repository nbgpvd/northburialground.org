# ZEBEDEE HOWLAND

Sergeant Major, 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery (Colored). Barber of Providence. Died March 13, 1883. Reinterred here September 8, 1886.

## Life

Zebedee Howland was a barber in Providence for more than thirty years. [3][4] In 1857 he drilled with the Providence National Guards, a Black militia company the State of Rhode Island refused to recognize. [5]

He enlisted in the 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery (Colored) as a private on August 27, 1863. [8] On November 5 he was appointed sergeant major, the senior enlisted soldier of the regiment. [9]

In June 1864, at Carrollton, Louisiana, a court-martial of white officers tried him on two charges from one morning, June 5, at Camp Parapet. The first was that he failed to pass two orders from the acting adjutant, Lieutenant Zephaniah Brown, to two sergeants. The second was that, sent to carry the day's orders to each company, he came back before finishing and told Brown he ought to make out a copy for each captain, as they had no time to copy it. [10]

![The charges and specifications, as read at his arraignment. Court-martial case file LL 2229, National Archives. [10]](../../records/G3/nara-ll2229-charges.jpg)

Asked whether Howland's manner was disrespectful, Brown answered: "He gave me the idea that he was instructing me in my duties." [10]

![Lieutenant Brown's testimony. LL 2229. [10]](../../records/G3/nara-ll2229-brown-testimony.jpg)

Howland pleaded not guilty and submitted two statements over his signature. In the second he wrote that a captain had said to him, "we ought to have a copy of these orders"; that he went back at once and told the adjutant so; and that Brown "told me to go to my Quarters under arrest." [10]

![Howland's statement in answer to the second charge, signed "Zebedee Howland, Sergeant Major." LL 2229. [10]](../../records/G3/nara-ll2229-statement-b.jpg)

The court found him guilty on both charges and sentenced him "to be reduced to the ranks, and forfeit all pay for a period of two months." Brigadier General T. W. Sherman approved the sentence on June 29. [10][11]

![Findings, sentence and approval, June 29, 1864. LL 2229. [10]](../../records/G3/nara-ll2229-sentence.jpg)

Twelve days after the reduction took effect, his company made him a sergeant again. [8] In December, at Fort Jackson, his captain had him reduced a second time, by order and without a hearing, "for incompetency and continual neglect of duty." [12]

![Special Orders No. 36, Fort Jackson, December 15, 1864: "At the request of Capt. J. W. Fry, Co. A, Sergt. Zebedee Howland is hereby reduced to the ranks." Regimental order book, National Archives. [12]](../../records/G3/nara-so36-1864.jpg)

In July 1865 a regimental court fined him half a month's pay for absence without leave; he pleaded guilty. [13] He mustered out on October 2, 1865, a private. [8]

![General Orders No. 6, Brashear City, July 1865, item VII: "Private Zebedee Howland of Co. A … Charge absent without leave." Regimental order book, National Archives. [13]](../../records/G3/nara-go6-1865.jpg)

In Providence he returned to his shop. [3] He rose to major commanding the Burnside National Guards, a Black militia company; in 1875 the State enrolled the Guards in the Rhode Island Militia. [14][15][30] He belonged to Ives Post No. 13 of the Grand Army of the Republic, and on Decoration Day 1875 was one of the state G.A.R.'s assistant inspectors in the parade. [16]

His wife Esther died on January 31, 1883. [28][17] On March 13 he died suddenly at his house, 44 Union Street. A newspaper gave the cause as heart disease. [24] The City buried him at its own expense in the Colored Free Ground. A state law of 1885 required the City to move honorably discharged soldiers out of pauper ground. [25] On September 8, 1886, his body was raised and reburied here, in the Prescott Post lot. The clerk wrote one word beside the order: "Soldier." [2]

![The City's burial ledger, March 1883: Howland's struck entry, ditto marks for "Colored Free Ground 555," and the red note "Soldier. Raised Sept. 8/86 & Int. in Prov. G.A.R. Lot Grave 23. Chg. City." [See the whole page](../../records/G3/1883-burial-ledger.jpg). Courtesy City of Providence, Department of Parks, North Burial Ground. [2]](../../records/G3/1883-burial-ledger-detail.jpg)

His stone gives his company and regiment. It does not give his rank. [18]

## A reading of the record

*What follows is interpretation by the researcher, not a finding in the documents.*

Three months before Howland's trial, in March–April 1864, non-commissioned officers of this regiment at Matagorda Island, Texas, were broken in rank for a silent protest at roll call over unequal pay. [19] Howland's case file does not mention Matagorda or pay. The connection rests on chronology and on the same officers appearing in both episodes. Readers should weigh it as implied, not stated. [10]

The three punishments of 1864–65 descend in process as the man descends in rank: a general court over a remark; then no court at all; then the lowest court for a day's absence. [10][12][13]

Providence then gave back what the Army had taken. The rank came from a Black militia company, and in 1875 the State recognized that company, as it had refused to recognize his first one in 1857. [5][14][15]

## The stone

ZEBEDEE HOWLAND / Co. A. 14th R.I.H.Arty. / Died March 13, 1883.

Rectangular white marble, government pattern; chipped and weathered; legible. Photograph IMG_1211 (B. Brennan, 2026). [18]

The stone gives his company and omits the rank of sergeant major. He held that rank for eight months and was mustered out a private, so the stone is not wrong; it records his discharge grade. [8][9] Who ordered it, and on what form, is not known.

## What we don't know

- Where in Rhode Island he was born. Both June 1863 enrollment lists and the 1870 and 1880 censuses give Rhode Island. The 1883 death registration index gives Providence; the image has not been seen. [6][32][33][34]
- His birth year. The records put it anywhere from about 1825 to 1833; see the conflict note under Sources. [1][2][6][24][33][34]
- Why the City ledger gives 52 Meeting Street. The death notice says he died at his house, 44 Union Street, and that is followed here; the ledger field may be an informant's or undertaker's address. His wife died six weeks earlier at 12 Manning Street, the street of the 1880 census and of the 1878 and 1880 deeds, so the family's own address is a third candidate. [2][21][24][28][34]
- His parents. "Son of" Lydia A. Howland Brown (d. 1844) and grandson of Philip Howland (d. 1820) are a working hypothesis from death notices. The 1883 Rhode Island death registration index names his mother as Lydia; the image has not been seen. [1][32]
- Whether he served in the 11th Rhode Island. The only evidence is a note on the June 1863 2nd District enrollment list, which gives his former service as "5 mos in 11th R.I. Reg." and his trade as butcher, of Cranston. No muster roll or other record corroborates it, and whether he was a soldier, a civilian employee or neither is not known. The 11th R.I. rolls are still to be searched. [6]
- Whether he was drawn in the draft. In June 1863 he was enrolled in Class I in two districts: as a barber of Manning Street in the 1st and as a butcher of Cranston in the 2nd. An enrollment list is not a draft record, and no record of the July 1863 draft drawing has been found. He enlisted on August 27. [6][8]
- Where he was from January to May 1864: with Company A at Matagorda Island, or on the regimental staff at New Orleans. [1]
- When he joined Ives Post, and the date of his Masonic membership. He was a member by May 1875. [16]
- When he held the majority of the Burnside National Guards. He was "Maj. Z. Howland" in October 1871, and on November 13, 1873, the clerk of the marriage register entered him as "Major" where his given name belonged. In May 1875 the Guards marched under Col. J. M. Munroe, and Howland marched with Ives Post. [14][16][30]
- Whether he held municipal office. The claim has circulated; no document supports it. It is not on this page.
- Who identified him as a soldier for the 1886 removal, and whether Ives Post or Prescott Post asked for it. The 1885 law and the City's enlargement of the lot in June 1886 explain the year; the ledger records the move and the City's charge; neither names who asked. The Prescott Post records at the Rhode Island Historical Society should. [2][25][26]
- Who "Christiana" in his 1880 household was. She is entered as his wife, 50, born in Maryland, on Manning Street. *Inference, not proven:* she is probably Esther under another name, or an enumerator's error. Esther was born in Maryland, was about the same age (55 at her death in 1883), died on Manning Street, and was still "Wife of Zebedee" in January 1883. No record of a Christiana Howland has been found. [28][34]
- No living direct descendant of Zebedee Howland has been found. ⟨BB: cite the descendant trace — where it is kept⟩
- Whether he or Esther applied for a federal pension. No pension index entry has been cited.
