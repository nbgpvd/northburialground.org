# [CH]ARLES A. JACKSO[N]

Corporal, Co. B, 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery (Colored) · born Baltimore, 1838 · died Providence, November 1915

## Life

Charles A. Jackson was born in Baltimore, Maryland, about 1838; the 1905 state census gives July 4, 1838. [4][11] He was a waiter in Providence when he married Frances A. Gordon, a Providence-born widow, on February 3, 1863. [7]

On July 30, 1863, he volunteered for the "1st Colored Company Heavy Artillery," and he signed the papers himself. [4] The *Providence Daily Journal* reported that he was the first man to enlist in that company, and that he worked so hard to fill its ranks that he "lost his chance for preferment in it." He was transferred to the second company, the paper said, where he became orderly sergeant. [5] He was mustered in as a sergeant of Company B, 14th Rhode Island Heavy Artillery (Colored), on September 14, 1863. On January 1, 1864, he was reduced to private; the company book says "for incompetency." [4] A muster roll records that he had been free on April 19, 1861, the date that decided whether a Black soldier was owed equal pay from his enlistment. [4][15] He was present at every muster.

In August 1865, at Brashear City, Louisiana, his captain recommended him for corporal. He was mustered out a corporal at New Orleans on October 2, 1865. [4]

Back in Providence he worked as a laborer and teamster. [8][10][11] In June 1890 the census of Union veterans listed him at the State Workhouse and House of Correction; it does not say why. [9] From 1900 he drew an invalid pension. [13] He and Frances lived at 30 Penelope Place from at least 1900 to 1910. [10][11][12] He died at Rhode Island Hospital in November 1915, a widower, and was buried at North Burial Ground. The city register gives the 7th; his stone gives the 11th. [14][1]

## The stone

[CH]ARLES A. JACKSO[N] / Co. B. 11 U.S. H. Art. / Died Nov. 11, 1915

Transcription as recorded in the plot register from the photograph; square brackets mark letters not legible or below ground. Photographed November 11, 2026. [1]

The stone names the regiment by its last title, the 11th U.S. Colored Heavy Artillery, announced August 28, 1864. [6] It gives no rank. Its date of death, November 11, differs from the city death register's 7th. [14]

## What we don't know

- When did he die? The city death register gives the 7th (entry 25; our copy of the page does not show the month), the stone the 11th. [14][1] Was the 11th his burial day? The City's interment record should say.
- Where exactly is his grave recorded? The City lot record for C1 has not yet been matched.
- Why was he reduced from sergeant? The company book says "for incompetency" and nothing more. [4] The muster-out roll gives the end of his sergeancy as September 24, 1864; both descriptive book cards and the January–February 1864 muster roll give January 1, 1864, and those are followed here. [4]
- Why was he at the State Workhouse and House of Correction in June 1890, and was he held there or working there? The schedule does not say. [9]
- What does his pension file say? The index gives application 1,244,078 and certificate 1,074,930, filed February 13, 1900, and a later file number, C 2,519,387. [13] The file has not been read.
- Is he the Charles Jackson, 32, steamboat hand, born Rhode Island, living with Frances Jackson and Elizabeth Taylor in Providence's 6th Ward in 1870? Frances's mother was Elizabeth Taylor [7], but the birthplace and occupation do not fit him, and the regiment had a second Charles A. Jackson, in Company E, born in Albany, New York. [6]
- When did Frances die, and where is she buried? She is not in the death register entry; he was a widower by 1915. [14]
- Who was Frances's first husband, Gordon? The 1863 register records the marriage to Jackson as her second. [7]
- A list of North Burial Ground veterans compiled in 2022 gives his death as March 29, 1905; the stone and the death register both put it in 1915. [3][1][14]
